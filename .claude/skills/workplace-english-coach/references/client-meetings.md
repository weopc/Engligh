# Client Meetings — Phrases & Cultural Notes

For Western (often Swedish/Northern European) clients in Optimizely consulting context. Always lean toward **direct but warm** — over-politeness reads as evasive in Western business culture.

## Cultural quick-reference

| Vietnamese instinct | Western client expectation |
|---|---|
| Long context before the ask | State the ask first, context after |
| "Maybe we should consider…" (soft hint) | "I'd recommend X because Y" (clear position) |
| Avoiding "no" / saying "we'll try" | Clear trade-offs: "we can do X by Friday, or Y by Wednesday — which matters more?" |
| Apologizing for asking questions | Questions = professionalism, not rudeness |
| Silence = uncomfortable, fill it | Silence = thinking, let it breathe |
| "I think maybe…" hedging | Confident framing with caveats: "Based on what we've seen, X" |

## Phrase banks

### Kickoff / opening a meeting
- **"Thanks for making time. Let's jump in."** — confident, no fluff opener
- **"Before we start, I want to make sure we cover X, Y, Z — does that align with what you had in mind?"** — setting agenda + giving control
- **"Quick context for anyone new: I'm [name], tech lead on the [project] side from Niteco."** — clear self-intro

### Asking for clarification (without sounding lost)
- **"Just to make sure I'm tracking — when you say X, do you mean A or B?"** — sounds analytical, not confused
- **"Could you walk me through the use case one more time?"** — natural, not apologetic
- **"I want to play that back to make sure I got it: ..."** — shows you're listening actively
- ⚠️ Avoid: "Sorry, I don't understand" — sounds underprepared. Use the framings above.

### Pushing back on scope / timeline
The hardest move for intermediate speakers. Avoid both extremes (too soft = client doesn't hear the no; too blunt = sounds rude).

- **Soft**: "That's doable, but it would push the [other feature] out by a sprint. Is that the trade-off you'd want?"
- **Neutral**: "We can do that, but I want to flag two things first: [risk 1], [risk 2]. Still want to go ahead?"
- **Firm**: "I wouldn't recommend that approach. Here's why: [reason]. What I'd suggest instead is [X]."
- **Formal/written**: "Based on the current sprint capacity, adding this would impact the [feature] delivery. Could we discuss prioritization?"

### Explaining technical concepts to non-tech clients
- **"Think of it like…"** — analogies land well
- **"The short version is…"** — signals you'll abstract; then offer depth: **"happy to go deeper if useful"**
- **"There's a trade-off here: [option A] is faster but [downside]; [option B] is more robust but [downside]."** — Western clients love trade-off framing
- ⚠️ Avoid dumping jargon (Optimizely-specific terms) without checking. Say: **"Quick check — are you familiar with [term], or want me to explain it?"**

### Disagreeing politely but clearly
- **"I see where you're coming from, but I'd push back on that — here's my concern: …"** — the classic. Memorize it.
- **"That could work, but have you considered…"** — opens space without confrontation
- **"I have a slightly different read on this. To me, …"** — claims your view as valid

### Handling unclear/vague client direction
- **"Help me understand the priority here — is X more important, or Y?"**
- **"What does success look like in 3 months?"** — reframes vague asks into concrete outcomes
- **"Can we narrow this down a bit? If we had to ship one thing first, what would it be?"**

### Wrapping up / next steps
- **"Let me recap what I'm hearing: [A], [B], [C]. Anything I missed?"**
- **"On my side, I'll [action] by [date]. Can you [their action] by [date]?"** — explicit ownership
- **"I'll send a follow-up with the decisions and action items."** — sets expectations

### When you don't know the answer
- **"I don't have the full picture on that — let me check with [team/spec] and get back to you by [time]."** — far better than guessing or "I'll try"
- **"Good question — I want to give you a proper answer rather than guess. Can I follow up tomorrow?"**

### When the client interrupts or talks over you
- **"Sorry, can I finish that thought?"** — direct, fine in Western culture
- **"Let me just close on this point, then I'd love to hear yours."**

## Sentence stress / delivery notes
- For Vietnamese speakers: lean into **falling intonation** at the end of statements (avoid rising — sounds uncertain).
- Pause before key words rather than rushing. Western clients read pauses as confidence.
- "Sorry" early in a sentence weakens the whole sentence. Reserve it for actual apologies.
