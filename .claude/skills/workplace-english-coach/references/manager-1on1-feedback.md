# Manager 1:1s, Feedback, Mentoring

Covers three related skills: talking with your manager, giving/receiving feedback, mentoring juniors. All require **direct-but-warm** English — a register that's specifically hard for Vietnamese speakers because Vietnamese workplace norms favor hierarchy-respecting indirection.

## Talking with your manager

### Asking for clarity on expectations
- **"I want to make sure we're aligned on what good looks like for [project] — can we walk through that?"**
- **"What does a strong outcome here look like in your eyes?"**
- **"If you were in my seat, what would you prioritize this week?"** — useful when overloaded

### Flagging issues / blockers / overload
- **"I want to give you a heads-up: [issue]. Here's what I'm thinking [plan]. Does that make sense to you?"**
- **"I'm running into a capacity issue — I have A, B, and C on my plate. Which would you want me to deprioritize?"** — forces the trade-off conversation, far better than just complaining
- **"I want to flag a risk early rather than late: …"** — signals proactiveness

### Disagreeing with your manager
- **"I see it a bit differently — can I share my perspective?"**
- **"I want to push back on this, gently — here's why: …"** — "gently" softens it, "push back" still gets the point across
- **"I hear you, and I want to make sure I understand before I disagree — are you saying [X]?"** — buys you time and shows respect

### Career / growth conversations
- **"I'd like to set aside 15 minutes in our next 1:1 to talk about my growth — would that work?"** — frame it as a calendar request, not a vulnerable ask
- **"Looking ahead 6-12 months, what would you want to see from me to take on more?"**
- **"I'm interested in moving toward [architect / staff / management] — what would help me get there?"**

### Asking for feedback
- **"What's one thing I could be doing better?"** — specific number forces real answer
- **"I'd love direct feedback — don't sugarcoat it."** — explicitly invites directness, often needed with managers who default to politeness
- **"On [recent project], where do you think I missed the mark?"** — specific context = useful feedback

## Giving feedback (to juniors or peers)

### The structure that works in English
1. **Specific observation** (not vague judgment)
2. **Impact** (why it matters)
3. **Suggestion / question** (forward-looking)

Example:
> "In yesterday's PR, the controller had ~300 lines mixing validation and business logic *(observation)*. That makes it hard for the next person to test and extend *(impact)*. Would you try splitting it into a validator + service layer? Happy to pair on it *(suggestion + offer)*."

### Positive feedback (don't skip this)
Vietnamese instinct often skips praise. In Western teams, **silent = displeased**.

- **"I want to call out — the way you handled [X] yesterday was really solid."**
- **"Nice work on [Y]. Specifically, [the part that was good]."** — specific praise > generic
- **"That was a good catch."** — small, frequent praise

### Corrective feedback (without crushing the person)
- **"Can I share something I noticed?"** — asks permission, lowers defensiveness
- **"I want to flag something for next time."** — future-focused
- **"This is a small thing, but worth getting right: …"** — calibrates severity
- ⚠️ Avoid: "You always…" / "You never…" — triggers defensiveness in any culture, but especially harsh in English

### Hard feedback (performance issue)
- **"I want to be direct with you about something."** — signals seriousness
- **"This is hard to say but important — your [behavior] is impacting [outcome]. Let's talk about what's going on and how to fix it."**
- **"I want this conversation to be useful, not just hard — what's your read on it?"** — invites their side

## Mentoring juniors

### Coaching mode (Socratic, not directive)
- **"What have you tried so far?"** — almost always the right first question
- **"Walk me through your thinking."**
- **"What would happen if [edge case]?"** — pushes them to think deeper
- **"What's your gut tell you here?"** — builds judgment

### When they're stuck
- **"Let's break this down. What's the first thing you'd need to know?"**
- **"You're closer than you think — what's the blocker?"**

### When they're overconfident on something risky
- **"Help me understand why this approach over [alternative]?"** — non-judgmental, forces justification
- **"What's the failure mode here? If this breaks in production at 2am, what would the on-call see?"** — concrete reframe

### Encouraging
- **"You're growing fast — keep this trajectory."**
- **"Don't underestimate how much you've leveled up in the last [3 months]."**
- **"I'd trust you on [X] now — that wouldn't have been true 6 months ago."**

## Receiving feedback (a small but high-leverage skill)

When someone gives you feedback, **avoid these reflexes**:
- ❌ Immediately explaining/defending (sounds defensive even if you mean to clarify)
- ❌ Over-apologizing ("So sorry, I'm so sorry…") — uncomfortable for Western givers
- ❌ Going silent

Instead:
- ✅ **"Thanks for telling me — let me sit with that for a second."** — pause is OK
- ✅ **"That's useful. Can you give me a specific example so I can picture it?"**
- ✅ **"Yeah, I see it. I'll work on [concrete change]."** — owns it cleanly
- ✅ **"I want to push back a little on one part — [reason]. But on [other part], you're right."** — partial agreement is valid

## Phrases to *retire* (Vietnamese intermediate-speaker overuse)

- ❌ "I'll try my best" → ✅ "I'll have it done by Friday" or "I'll see what's realistic and let you know by EOD"
- ❌ "Sorry for the late reply" (every time) → reserve for actually late, not slightly late
- ❌ "Please kindly review" → ✅ "Could you take a look when you have a moment?"
- ❌ "I will be appreciate" → ✅ "I'd appreciate it" or just drop the phrase
- ❌ "Noted" as a standalone reply → ✅ "Got it, will do" or "Thanks — I'll follow up after [action]"
