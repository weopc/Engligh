# Vietnamese → English Common Traps

Patterns where direct translation from Vietnamese produces unnatural or wrong English. Watch for these in **Polish mode** and surface them as quick tips during **Practice mode**.

## Tense & aspect

Vietnamese doesn't grammatically mark tense the way English does. This produces predictable errors:

- ❌ "Yesterday I go to office." → ✅ "Yesterday I went to the office."
- ❌ "I working on this feature." → ✅ "I'm working on this feature."
- ❌ "I do it already." → ✅ "I've already done it." / "Already done."
- ❌ "When you come, we discuss." → ✅ "When you come, we'll discuss." / "When you arrive, we'll discuss it."

**Rule of thumb**: every English sentence must have a tense. If unsure, default to present continuous ("I'm doing X") for now, simple past ("I did X") for done, and "will" for future commitments.

## Articles (a / an / the)

Vietnamese has no articles. Errors:

- ❌ "I send you email." → ✅ "I'll send you **an** email." / "I'll send you **the** email."
- ❌ "I review PR for you." → ✅ "I'll review **the** PR for you."
- ❌ "Client want new feature." → ✅ "**The** client wants **a** new feature."

**Quick rule**:
- First mention of a countable thing: **a/an**
- Subsequent mentions or specific known thing: **the**
- Concepts / uncountable / plurals talking generally: no article

## Plurals

- ❌ "We have three issue." → ✅ "We have three issue**s**."
- ❌ "Many bug in this PR." → ✅ "Many bug**s** in this PR." / "A lot of bugs in this PR."

## Subject-verb agreement

- ❌ "He don't agree." → ✅ "He doesn't agree."
- ❌ "The team have decided." → ✅ "The team has decided." (American) / Either works in British English

## Over-politeness / over-formality

Vietnamese workplace politeness translated literally sounds robotic or evasive:

- ❌ "Please kindly review my PR." → ✅ "Could you review my PR when you get a chance?"
- ❌ "I would like to humbly ask…" → ✅ "Quick question:" / "Could you help me with…"
- ❌ "Sorry for bothering you." (when not actually bothering) → ✅ Just ask. Or "Hey, got a sec?"
- ❌ "I will try my best." → ✅ "I'll have it done by Friday." (commit to specifics, not effort)

## Direct translation idioms

- ❌ "Open camera" (mở camera) → ✅ "Turn on the camera" / "Turn your camera on"
- ❌ "Open the door" is fine, but: "Open the meeting" → ✅ "Start the meeting" / "Kick off the meeting"
- ❌ "Close the issue" is technically fine but: "Close the laptop" → ✅ "Shut the laptop"
- ❌ "Big problem" (vấn đề lớn) → often ✅ "Major issue" / "Big concern" depending on context
- ❌ "Eat lunch" is fine. "Eat coffee" → ✅ "Grab a coffee" / "Have a coffee"

## Prepositions (high-error area)

- ❌ "Deploy **on** production" → ✅ "Deploy **to** production"
- ❌ "Discuss **about** something" → ✅ "Discuss something" (no "about") OR "Talk **about** something"
- ❌ "Listen music" → ✅ "Listen **to** music"
- ❌ "Married **with** someone" → ✅ "Married **to** someone"
- ❌ "On the meeting" → ✅ "**In** the meeting"
- ❌ "In Monday" → ✅ "**On** Monday"
- ❌ "Depend **of**" → ✅ "Depend **on**"
- ❌ "Different **with**" → ✅ "Different **from**" (American) / "Different **to**" (British)

## Singular "they" and gender-neutral language

Modern English defaults to "they/them" when gender is unknown or irrelevant:

- ❌ "When a developer push his code…" → ✅ "When a developer pushes their code…"

## False friends / words with different scope

- **"Actually"** in English = "in fact, contrary to expectation" — NOT "currently / right now". For "hiện tại / bây giờ" use **"currently"** or **"right now"**.
- **"Sympathy"** ≠ "thông cảm" in most contexts. To say "I understand your position", use **"I understand"** or **"that makes sense"**, not "I have sympathy".
- **"Sensible"** = wise, practical (NOT sensitive/cảm xúc). For emotional: **"sensitive"**.
- **"Eventually"** = "after a long time, finally" — NOT "if needed / possibly". For "có thể" use **"possibly"** / **"if needed"**.
- **"Convenient"** for a time = OK ("is Thursday convenient?"). But "I will convenient" is wrong — use "it works for me" / "that works".

## Sentence structure habits

### Topic-prominent → subject-prominent
Vietnamese is topic-prominent. English needs subject-verb at the start.

- ❌ "This bug, I fix it tomorrow." → ✅ "I'll fix this bug tomorrow." / "I'll get to this bug tomorrow."
- ❌ "About the deployment, we decide later." → ✅ "We'll decide about the deployment later." / "Let's circle back on the deployment."

### Over-hedging
- ❌ "Maybe I think possibly we could try…" → ✅ "Let's try X." (pick one hedge max, or none)

### Repeating the subject pronoun
- ❌ "The client, he wants X, he doesn't like Y." → ✅ "The client wants X but doesn't like Y."

## Phrase patterns worth memorizing (these fix many errors at once)

- **"Let me…"** — Let me check / let me think / let me get back to you. Far more natural than "I will check".
- **"I'll have to…"** — softens "I must / I need to" while keeping commitment.
- **"That makes sense."** — universal acknowledgment, way better than "Yes I understand".
- **"Got it."** — quick acknowledgment.
- **"Fair point."** — agreeing with someone's argument without fully conceding.
- **"Quick one —"** — natural opener for short asks.
- **"To be honest,"** / **"Honestly,"** — signals candid opinion coming.
