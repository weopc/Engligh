# Slack & Email — Async Communication

Async writing has different rules from speech. The most common Vietnamese-intermediate mistakes: **too formal in Slack** (sounds robotic) and **too informal in client email** (sounds careless). Match the channel.

## Channel register guide

| Channel | Register | Example greeting |
|---|---|---|
| Slack with team | Casual | "Hey", "Yo", or just dive in |
| Slack with manager | Slightly more careful | "Hey [name]" + clear ask |
| Slack with client | Professional-casual | "Hi [name]" |
| Email to team | Light formal | "Hi team" |
| Email to client | Formal-friendly | "Hi [name]," |
| Email to multiple clients / new contact | Formal | "Hello [name]," |

## Slack patterns

### Opening a message (don't waste the first line)
- **"Quick question:"** then the question
- **"Heads-up:"** then the FYI
- **"Need your input on something —"** then the ask
- **"Update on [project]:"** then the update

Avoid: "Hi, hope you're doing well, I wanted to ask…" — Slack readers skim. Lead with the ask.

### Making a request
- **"Can you take a look at [link] when you get a chance?"** — soft, default
- **"Could I get your eyes on this PR? No rush, by EOD tomorrow is fine."** — specific deadline
- **"Need a quick decision from you on [X] — leaning [option], any concerns?"** — speeds up async decisions

### Following up
- **"Bumping this — any thoughts?"** — neutral, no guilt-trip
- **"Following up on this when you have a sec."**
- **"Hey, this is getting time-sensitive — could you take a look today?"** — escalating with reason

### Saying no / declining
- **"Can't take this on right now — already at capacity with [X]. Could it wait until [date]?"**
- **"I'd skip it — here's why: [reason]. But happy to be overruled."**
- **"Not the right person for this — try [name], they own that area."**

### Acknowledging
- **"Got it, thanks — will do."**
- **"Makes sense, going with that."**
- **"On it."** — short and good
- **"Thanks for the context."**
- Avoid bare "Noted." — reads cold in English (different from Vietnamese)

### Apologizing (only when warranted)
- **"My bad — fixed."** — small mistake, casual
- **"Sorry about the delay — got pulled into [thing]."** — useful for actual lateness
- ⚠️ Don't open every reply with "sorry" if you're not actually late

### Async standup / status
- **"Yesterday: shipped [X]. Today: [Y]. Blocker: waiting on [Z] from [team]."** — bullet format works
- **"Quick status: [project] is on track for [date], one yellow flag on [specific risk]."**

## Email patterns

### Subject lines (clarity beats cleverness)
- **"Question on [feature] — need answer by Thu"**
- **"[Project] kickoff — agenda + pre-read"**
- **"Heads up: API changes in next release"**
- **"Action needed: confirm [X] before Friday"**

### Opening (one line, get to the point)
- **"Hi [name],"** then **"I wanted to follow up on [X]."**
- **"Hi [name] — quick question on [topic]."**
- ⚠️ Avoid: "I hope this email finds you well." Native speakers cringe. If you want warmth, ask about something specific: "Hope your week is going OK — quick question:"

### The ask (state it clearly, early)
- **"Could you let me know [X] by [date]?"**
- **"I'd like to propose [X]. Two options: [A] or [B]. What's your preference?"**
- **"We need a decision on [X] by [date] to stay on track for [milestone]."**

### Context (just enough, then stop)
1-3 sentences. If you need more than that, you probably need a meeting.

### Closing
- **"Let me know if anything's unclear."** — open invitation
- **"Looking forward to your thoughts."**
- **"Happy to jump on a call if easier."**
- Sign-off: **"Thanks,"** / **"Best,"** / **"Cheers,"** (cheers is friendly-casual, good with familiar clients)

## Common email/Slack templates

### "I can't make the meeting"
> Hi [name], I won't be able to make the [meeting] on [date] — got a conflict. Could we reschedule, or would async work? Happy to send my input ahead of time if useful.

### "I need to push a deadline"
> Hi [name], I want to flag early: [task] is going to slip from [original date] to [new date]. Reason: [brief, no excuses]. Here's the recovery plan: [next steps]. Let me know if this creates a problem on your end.

### "Following up on no response"
> Hi [name], bumping this one — no rush, but I want to make sure it didn't slip. Original ask was: [one-line restatement]. Thanks!

### "Saying you'll get back to someone"
> Hi [name], thanks for sending this over — I want to give it a proper look. Will get back to you by [specific time]. 

### "Closing the loop after a task is done"
> Hi [name], wanted to close the loop on [task] — [outcome]. [One-line context if needed.] Let me know if anything else needed on this.

## Style rules

- **One ask per message** when possible. If you need 3 things, number them.
- **Specific deadlines** (`by Thursday EOD`) beat vague ones (`when you can`).
- **Bullet > paragraph** for anything with >2 items.
- **Don't bury the lede**. The most important sentence goes first or near the top.
- **Re-read before sending**. If you're a non-native writer, this single habit beats most other improvements.
