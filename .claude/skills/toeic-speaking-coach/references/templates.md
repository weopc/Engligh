# TOEIC Speaking — Templates, Frames & Vietnamese Pitfalls

Use these to teach structure in Strategy mode and to build the "model answer (band cao)" in drills. Templates are scaffolding — push the user toward natural variation once they're comfortable.

## Q1–2 Read a text aloud

No template (the text is given). Coach **delivery**:
- **Thought groups**: chunk the sentence and pause (/) at commas and clause boundaries. *"For a limited time, / our store / is offering / a special discount / on all kitchen appliances."*
- **Content-word stress**: stress nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs; unstress articles, prepositions, auxiliaries. Mark with CAPS when coaching: *"our STORE is OFfering a SPEcial DIScount."*
- **Rising vs falling intonation**: rise on list items and yes/no questions, fall at the end of statements.
- Read for **45s** — don't rush; pace ≈ 130–150 wpm.

### 🔊 Vietnamese pronunciation pitfalls to flag (always remind user to record)
- **Final consonants** dropped or softened: *advised, products, helped* — the final /d/, /s/, /t/ must be audible.
- **-ed endings**: /t/ (helped), /d/ (advised), /ɪd/ (started). VN speakers often drop these.
- **-s/-es endings** (plural / 3rd person): *appliances, offers* — don't omit.
- **/θ/ /ð/** (think, this) often become /t/ /d/.
- **Consonant clusters**: *strengths, asked* — don't insert vowels or drop sounds.
- **Word stress on longer words**: *apPLIance, disCOUNT (n. DIScount), comMUnicate, opPORtunity.*
- **/l/ vs /n/** and final **/l/** (*available, detail*).

## Q3–4 Describe a picture

**Frame (aim 4–6 sentences in 30s):**
1. **Overview**: "This is a picture of [place/situation]." / "This picture was taken in/at…"
2. **Main subject + action (use present continuous)**: "In the center/foreground, a man is …-ing."
3. **Other people/objects**: "On the left/right, there are … / I can see …"
4. **Detail**: number, color, clothing, expression, weather, what's on tables/walls.
5. **Inference/closer**: "It looks like … / They seem to be … / It appears to be [a meeting / morning]."

**Useful language**: *in the foreground / background, on the left/right, in the middle, next to, behind, in front of, there is / there are, wearing, holding, looking at, it looks like, they seem to be.*

### Pitfalls
- Listing nouns without verbs ("a man, a table, a computer") → use full sentences with **-ing** verbs.
- Forgetting **there is/there are** agreement.
- Over-describing one corner and running out of time — cover the whole scene.

## Q5–7 Respond to questions (familiar topic / survey)

**Q5–6 (15s, 1–2 sentences) — answer + one detail:**
- "Yes, I usually … because …" / "The last time I … was …"
**Q7 (30s, 3–5 sentences) — opinion/recommendation + reasons:**
- "I'd recommend … for two reasons. First, … Also, … So I think …"

**Frames**: *Personally, I … / In my case … / I tend to … / What I like most is … / If I had to choose, I'd say …*

### Pitfalls
- One-word answers ("Yes." / "No.") → always add a reason or detail.
- Going silent to think — fillers are fine: *"Well, let me think… I'd say…"*
- Switching tense mid-answer.

## Q8–10 Respond to questions using information provided

Strategy: in the 45s reading time, **locate names, times, dates, room/location, and any change/cancellation** in the schedule. Answer **exactly** what's asked, in full sentences.
- Q8–9 (15s): one fact, restated. "The keynote starts at 9 a.m. in Room A."
- Q10 (30s): often two facts or a short summary. "There are two sessions in the afternoon: first … at 1 p.m., and then … at 3 p.m."

**Frames**: *According to the schedule, … / The … is scheduled for … / Yes/No, actually … / There are two sessions: … and …*

### Pitfalls
- Reading the wrong row/time → accuracy caps the score, so verify before speaking.
- Reading the text verbatim in a flat voice → rephrase into a spoken answer.

## Q11 Express an opinion (the big one — 0–5)

**60-second template:**
1. **Position (1 sentence)**: "In my opinion, [restate the choice]." / "I strongly believe that…"
2. **Reason 1 + example (2 sentences)**: "The main reason is … For example, …"
3. **Reason 2 + example (2 sentences)**: "Another reason is … In my own experience, …"
4. **Restate (1 sentence)**: "That's why I think …"

**High-scoring connectors**: *The main reason is…, For instance…, In my experience…, On top of that…, That said…, Ultimately…, For these reasons…*

**Worked example** — *Prompt: Do you prefer working from home or in an office?*
> "In my opinion, working from home is more effective for me. The main reason is focus — at home I have fewer interruptions, so I can finish deep technical work faster. For example, when I review code, I need long quiet blocks, and that's hard in a busy office. Another reason is the time I save on commuting; I can use that time to rest or plan my day. That said, I still go to the office for important meetings. Ultimately, a flexible mix works best, but if I had to choose one, I'd choose working from home."

### Pitfalls
- No clear position, or flip-flopping → state it in the first sentence and stick to it.
- Reasons without examples → always attach a concrete example (work life is great material for this user).
- Running out of time before the restatement → keep reasons tight.

## General Vietnamese→English speaking traps (all tasks)
- **Tense agreement**: keep past/present consistent within an answer.
- **Articles** (a/an/the) and **plural -s** — frequently dropped.
- **Subject-verb agreement** with 3rd person -s.
- **Direct translation** of VN structure ("I very like" → "I really like"; "Although…, but…" → drop the "but").
- **Over-formality** — natural contractions (I'd, it's, there's) sound more fluent than stiff full forms.
- **Word stress** carries meaning in English far more than in Vietnamese — wrong stress can make a correct word unintelligible.
