# TOEIC Speaking — Scoring Rubrics & Score Mapping

Read this before scoring any response. Score honestly against these criteria, then explain in Vietnamese.

## Per-task raw scoring

### Q1–2 Read a text aloud (0–3 each)
Rated on **pronunciation, intonation, and stress**. (Grammar/vocab not assessed — the text is given.)
- **3** — Highly intelligible; appropriate stress, intonation, pausing at phrase boundaries; natural pace.
- **2** — Generally intelligible; some errors in stress/intonation/pace that don't block understanding.
- **1** — Frequent errors that interfere; choppy or monotone; mispronunciations strain the listener.
- **0** — No response / not in English.

> ⚠️ Text-interface limit: you cannot truly score this. Instead, mark word stress (CAP the stressed syllable), show thought-group pauses (/), flag likely VN pitfalls (final consonants, -ed/-s endings, /θ/ /ð/, word stress on long words), and tell the user to record and self-check.

### Q3–4 Describe a picture (0–3 each)
Rated on pronunciation, intonation, stress **plus grammar, vocabulary, and cohesion**.
- **3** — Clear, sustained description; relevant detail; accurate grammar/vocab; well-connected.
- **2** — Adequate description; some errors; covers the main scene but limited detail or minor irrelevance.
- **1** — Limited; fragments; errors obscure meaning; very little detail.
- **0** — No response / unrelated / not in English.
Content target: cover **who/what/where**, then **action** (-ing), then **detail** (number, color, clothing, mood, background).

### Q5–7 Respond to questions (0–3 each)
Rated on relevance, completeness, plus grammar/vocab/pronunciation. No reading.
- **3** — Directly answers, sustained and coherent; appropriate detail for the time.
- **2** — Answers but underdeveloped, or minor errors; some hesitation.
- **1** — Minimal/partial answer; errors interfere; off-target.
- **0** — No response / unrelated / not in English.
Note: Q5–6 are short (15s — 1–2 sentences). Q7 is longer (30s — give a reason/example).

### Q8–10 Respond to questions using information provided (0–3 each)
Same scale as Q5–7, but the answer **must be accurate to the provided schedule/agenda**. Pulling the wrong fact caps the score even if the English is good.
- **3** — Accurate info + clear, complete answer.
- **2** — Mostly accurate; minor info error or underdeveloped.
- **1** — Wrong info or barely addresses the question.
- **0** — No response / unrelated / not in English.
Q10 often asks for two pieces of info or a short summary (30s).

### Q11 Express an opinion (0–5)
Rated on whether the opinion is **clear, sustained, well-organized, and supported**, plus grammar/vocab/pronunciation/cohesion.
- **5** — Clear position; sustained; well-developed reasons + specific examples; effective connectors; only minor errors.
- **4** — Clear position; mostly developed; good control; some lapses.
- **3** — Position stated; some support but uneven; noticeable errors or thin development.
- **2** — Position unclear or barely supported; errors interfere; underdeveloped.
- **1** — Minimal; mostly off-target or incoherent.
- **0** — No response / unrelated / not in English.
Target shape in 60s: **state opinion → reason 1 + example → reason 2 + example → quick restatement.**

## How to estimate a transcript score (text interface)

You only see the transcript, so:
1. Score **content, grammar, vocab, structure, cohesion, relevance, and info-accuracy** fully against the rubric.
2. For Q1–2 (and the pronunciation slice of Q3–11), **do not invent a pronunciation score**. Either (a) score only the assessable dimensions and say so, or (b) give a *range* and note that real pronunciation/fluency could move it. Always tell the user this slice depends on their recording.
3. Check the **length/time fit**: a 15s answer ≈ 2–3 sentences; 30s ≈ 4–6; 60s (Q11) ≈ 130–170 words / ~6–9 sentences. Far under target → cap the score; padding/repetition → note it.

## Raw → scaled (0–200) and CEFR — approximate

The real test uses ETS equating; treat this as a study estimate only.

- Sum the raw points. Max raw = (2×3 read) + (2×3 picture) + (3×3 respond) + (3×3 info) + (1×5 opinion) = **6 + 6 + 9 + 9 + 5 = 35**.
- Rough scaled estimate: **scaled ≈ round((raw / 35) × 200)** — then round to the nearest 10 (the report uses 10-point increments).
- Approximate CEFR / proficiency-level bands on the 0–200 scale:
  - **160–200** — Advanced (≈ C1) — fluent, well-organized, minimal errors.
  - **130–150** — Above-intermediate (≈ B2) — sustained, clear, occasional errors. *Realistic target for this user.*
  - **110–120** — Intermediate (≈ B1) — communicates but limited range/development.
  - **80–100** — Elementary (≈ A2).
  - **0–70** — Beginner (≈ A1).

When you report an estimate, always: give the number, the band, the **single highest-ROI** task type to improve, and a reminder that pronunciation/fluency (unscored here) shift the real result.
