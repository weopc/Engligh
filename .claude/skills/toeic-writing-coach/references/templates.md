# TOEIC Writing — Templates, Frames & Vietnamese Traps

Use these for Strategy mode and to build the "model (band cao)" in drills. Templates are scaffolding — push the user toward natural variation as they improve.

## Q1–5 Write a sentence based on a picture

Goal: **one** grammatical sentence that **describes the picture** and uses **both given words** (any form/order).

**Reliable patterns** (combine to fit the picture):
- *Subject + is/are + -ing + … (present continuous for actions):* "The man **is repairing** the car **because** it broke down." (words: *repair / because*)
- *There is / There are …:* "**There are** several boxes **next to** the door." (words: *box / next to*)
- *While / When clause:* "**While** the woman **is waiting**, she is reading a magazine." (words: *while / wait*)
- *…, so / but / because …:* "The room is dark, **so** someone **turned on** the light." (words: *so / turn on*)
- *Relative clause:* "The employee **who** is presenting **looks** confident." (words: *who / present*)

Tips: use **present continuous** for ongoing actions in the photo; make sure both words are used **correctly**, not just inserted; keep it to **one** sentence; check article + plural + subject-verb agreement before submitting.

## Q6–7 Respond to a written request (email)

**Step 0 — read the instruction line and list the required actions.** It usually says something like *"…be sure to give ONE piece of information, ask TWO questions, and make ONE request."* Do **all** of them. Missing one caps your score.

**Email skeleton:**
```
Dear [Name] / Hi [Name],

[Opening — acknowledge their email]
Thank you for your email about … / I'm writing in response to …

[Body — do each required action, clearly]
- Give info:   I'd like to let you know that …
- Ask question: Could you tell me whether … ?  /  I was wondering …
- Make request: Would it be possible to … ?  /  I'd appreciate it if you could …
- Suggest:      I'd suggest … / It might be a good idea to …
- Apologize:    I'm sorry for … / Please accept my apologies for …

[Closing]
I look forward to hearing from you. / Please let me know if you need anything else.

Best regards,
[Your name]
```

**Register guide (match the email):** customer/client/manager → formal (*Dear…, Could you…, I would appreciate…*); coworker/friend → semi-formal (*Hi…, Can you…, Let me know*). Don't mix a casual opening with a stiff body.

**Useful phrases by function:**
- Requesting: *Could you please… / Would it be possible to… / I'd appreciate it if…*
- Asking: *Could you tell me… / I'd like to know… / Do you know whether…*
- Giving info: *I'm pleased to inform you… / Just to let you know… / Please note that…*
- Declining politely: *Unfortunately, I won't be able to… / I'm afraid that…*
- Confirming: *I'd be happy to… / That works for me. / I can confirm that…*

## Q8 Opinion essay (~300 words, 30 min)

**Skeleton (4–5 paragraphs):**
```
INTRO (2–3 sentences)
  - Hook / restate the topic in your words
  - Clear THESIS: state your opinion plainly
    "In my opinion, … / I firmly believe that … / I strongly agree that …"

BODY 1 (4–5 sentences)
  - Reason 1 (topic sentence): "The most important reason is …"
  - Explain + SPECIFIC example: "For example, in my own experience, …"

BODY 2 (4–5 sentences)
  - Reason 2: "Another key reason is …"
  - Explain + example: "For instance, …"

(Optional BODY 3 / counter-argument)
  - "Some people argue that … However, …"

CONCLUSION (2–3 sentences)
  - Restate opinion in new words: "In conclusion, …"
  - Final thought: "For these reasons, I believe …"
```

**Connector bank:**
- Adding: *Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, On top of that*
- Examples: *For example, For instance, To illustrate, In my experience*
- Contrast: *However, On the other hand, Nevertheless, That said*
- Cause/effect: *As a result, Therefore, Consequently, This means that*
- Concluding: *In conclusion, To sum up, Overall, For these reasons*

**Thesis frames (pick a clear side):**
- Agree/disagree: *"I completely agree with the idea that … because …"*
- Preference: *"While both options have merits, I believe … is the better choice."*
- Two reasons signposted: *"There are two main reasons why I hold this view: first, … and second, …"*

**Worked mini-example (intro + one body) — Prompt: *"Do you agree that companies should let employees work remotely?"***
> "Remote work has become a common topic in modern workplaces. In my opinion, companies should allow employees to work remotely, because it improves both productivity and well-being. The most important reason is focus. When employees work from home, they face fewer interruptions and can complete demanding tasks more efficiently. For example, in my own job as a developer, I do my best work during long, quiet blocks of time, which are difficult to find in a busy office. …"

## Vietnamese→English writing traps (all tasks)
- **Articles** (a/an/the) and **plurals (-s)** — most common omissions. Check every noun.
- **Subject–verb agreement** (3rd person -s; *there is* vs *there are*).
- **Tense consistency** — don't drift between past and present within a paragraph.
- **"Although …, but …"** — English uses one connector, not both. ("Although it was late, **I** finished." not "…, **but** I finished.")
- **Direct translation**: *"I very like" → "I really like"; "according to me" → "in my opinion"; "make a question" → "ask a question."*
- **Run-ons / comma splices** — join clauses with *and/but/so/because* or a period, not a bare comma.
- **Word form**: choose the right part of speech (*"It is very benefit" → "It is very beneficial" / "It has many benefits"*).
- **Over-formality / wordiness** — TOEIC rewards clear, direct sentences over inflated ones.
- **Prepositions**: *depend **on**, interested **in**, responsible **for**, good **at**.*
