# TOEIC Writing — Scoring Rubrics & Score Mapping

Read this before scoring. Score honestly against these criteria, then explain in Vietnamese.

## Per-task raw scoring

### Q1–5 Write a sentence based on a picture (0–3 each)
Rated on **grammar** and **relevance of the sentence to the picture**, using the **two given words/phrases** (any form, any order).
- **3** — One sentence, grammatically correct, clearly relevant to the picture, both words used correctly.
- **2** — Both words used and relevant, but **one grammar error**.
- **1** — Multiple grammar errors, OR a word misused, OR weak relevance to the picture.
- **0** — A given word missing, not a sentence, irrelevant, or not in English.
Hard rules that cap the score: **both words must appear**, it must be **one sentence**, it must **describe the picture**.

### Q6–7 Respond to a written request (0–4 each)
Rated on whether all **required tasks** are completed, plus quality/variety of sentences, vocabulary, and organization. The prompt lists specific actions (e.g., "ask two questions and make one suggestion") — these are mandatory.
- **4** — All required tasks done; clear, well-organized, varied sentences; minor errors only; appropriate tone.
- **3** — All (or nearly all) tasks done; generally clear; some errors; adequate organization.
- **2** — **One required task missing**, OR errors that sometimes interfere; underdeveloped or disorganized.
- **1** — Most tasks missing / very limited / errors block meaning.
- **0** — No relevant response / not in English / ignores the email.
Note: missing even one required action usually caps you at **2**, no matter how good the English is. Check the instruction line first.

### Q8 Write an opinion essay (0–5)
Rated on whether the opinion is **clearly stated, well-organized, fully developed with reasons/examples**, with grammar/vocab range and accuracy. Target ~300 words.
- **5** — Clear, consistent opinion; well-organized (intro/body/conclusion); reasons developed with specific support; varied vocabulary and structures; only minor errors.
- **4** — Clear opinion; good organization; mostly developed; some errors; some range.
- **3** — Opinion present; some development but uneven; noticeable errors; limited range; possibly a bit short.
- **2** — Opinion unclear or thinly supported; weak organization; errors interfere; under-length.
- **1** — Minimal, mostly off-topic, or incoherent.
- **0** — No relevant response / not in English / copies the prompt.
Caps: clearly **off-topic** or badly **under-length** (well under ~150 words) pulls the score down hard regardless of accuracy.

## How to estimate from a written answer

You see the full text, so you can score every dimension. Be strict about:
1. **Task completion first** — for Q1–5, are both words present? For Q6–7, is every required action done? For Q8, on-topic and long enough? This gates the score before you look at polish.
2. **Error density** — count errors that block vs. errors that don't. Band drops come from meaning-blocking errors and missing content, not the odd article slip.
3. **Range** — repeated identical structures cap the top band even when accurate; reward variety in connectors, clause types, and vocabulary.

## Raw → scaled (0–200) and CEFR — approximate

Real test uses ETS equating; treat this as a study estimate only.

- Max raw = (5×3 sentences) + (2×4 emails) + (1×5 essay) = **15 + 8 + 5 = 28**.
- Rough estimate: **scaled ≈ round((raw / 28) × 200)**, rounded to the nearest 10.
- Approximate proficiency bands on the 0–200 scale:
  - **170–200** — Advanced (≈ C1): precise, varied, well-organized, near error-free.
  - **140–160** — Above-intermediate (≈ B2): clear and developed, occasional errors. *Realistic target for this user.*
  - **120–130** — Intermediate (≈ B1): communicates but limited range/development.
  - **90–110** — Elementary (≈ A2).
  - **0–80** — Beginner (≈ A1).

When you report an estimate, always give: the number, the band, the **single highest-ROI** task type to improve, and the recurring pattern most worth fixing next.
